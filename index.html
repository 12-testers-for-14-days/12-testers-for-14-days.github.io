The 14-day rule, explained

Why Google requires 12 testers for 14 days

On November 13, 2023, Google Play tightened its publishing rules. Any developer who created a personal developer account after that date must run a closed test with at least 12 opted-in testers for 14 continuous days before they can apply for production access. The rule was designed to stop low-quality apps from flooding the store — and it works, but it puts real friction on solo developers and indie studios.

What counts as a "real" tester?

Google is strict. A tester must be a real person who has opted in to your test through the Play Store, has a verified Google account, and uses a physical Android device. Emulators, bots, and shared accounts are flagged by Google's anti-fraud systems and can result in a permanent account suspension. The testers also need to actively use the app — opening it multiple times during the 14 days — to count as engaged.

What we do differently

Our network is built from real, opted-in users across 40+ countries. Each tester has a track record of legitimate Play Store activity and verified device ownership. When you place an order, we deliver 12 tester emails (more, on higher plans) that you add to your closed testing track. Our compliance team monitors opt-in and engagement throughout the 14-day window, and we send you a written report at the end.

Why you can't just ask your friends

You can, but it's risky. If your 12 friends all live in the same city, use the same Wi-Fi, or share an IP pattern, Google's systems may flag them as a coordinated test. We've seen rejections for exactly this. Our testers are geographically distributed, use their own devices, and behave like real users — which is what Google's algorithm rewards.

What happens if you skip the 14-day test

Your production access application gets rejected with the message "Insufficient testing." You then have to complete the 14-day test and re-apply, which adds 2–4 weeks to your launch. We've also seen accounts get suspended for repeat violations. The 14-day test is not optional — it's the gate to the store.

What is the 12 testers for 14 days Google Play requirement?
Since November 13, 2023, Google Play requires personal developer accounts to have their app tested by at least 12 opted-in testers for 14 continuous days before they can apply for production access. Organization accounts (with a D-U-N-S number) are exempt, but the closed testing track is still required for new personal accounts.
Are the testers real humans?
Yes. Every tester in our network is a real person using a physical Android device. We never use emulators, bots, or automated scripts. All testers are opted-in, geographically distributed, and have verified Google accounts with a track record of legitimate Play Store activity.
Does this work for personal developer accounts?
Yes — our service is specifically built for personal developer accounts that need to meet the 12 testers × 14 days requirement. It also works for organization accounts that want fast, reliable closed testing.
