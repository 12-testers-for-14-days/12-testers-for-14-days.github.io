The Bottleneck

The 14-day rule, done for you.

Google Play requires personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023, to run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in for 14 consecutive days before applying for production access.

The catch is continuity. The 14 days must be unbroken. If a tester uninstalls your app on day 10, the timer resets to zero. As Google states, "testers not being engaged" is the primary reason production access is refused. That is exactly what our managed service prevents.

  • Real testers, real devices. No emulators, no bots. Testing spans Android 7 to Android 15 across brands like Samsung, Pixel, and Xiaomi.
  • Continuous opt-in held. We keep your active count at or above 12 every single day, so the timer never resets.
  • Genuine engagement. Our testers actually open and use your app. We generate the vitals data Google's algorithm looks for.

Why DIY Fails

Finding 12 testers yourself is the hard part.

The requirement looks simple until you try it. Any one of these four common mistakes sends you back to Day 0.

Friends forget

Volunteers install on day one, then go quiet. The moment your active count slips under 12, the 14-day timer restarts.

Emulators get flagged

Emulator and bot installs from cheap forums are detected by the Play Integrity API and discarded. They lead to account strikes.

Dropouts reset the clock

The 14 days must be continuous. A single day below 12 opted-in testers means the entire two-week period starts over from scratch.

Silent installs flag

Testers who install but never open the app are marked as not engaged. Google checks for genuine usage, not just an install count.
